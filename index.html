About

I trained as a plant pathologist at the University of the Free State. My MSc looked at salicylic acid, Sclerotinia stem rot and the soybean root microbiome, and my PhD in Plant Health Ecology develops plant growth-promoting bacteria and biological control agents for sustainable soybean production.

At Afrikelp I lead the applied science behind the company's Ecklonia maxima seaweed products. I design protocols for field trials in the United States and greenhouse trials in South Africa, work with contract research organisations and universities, and support commercial teams and partners around the world. Alongside the trials, I study how seaweed extracts act in the plant at a biochemical and physiological level.

Before Afrikelp I worked in environmental science, running microbial bioreactors that treat contaminated mine water in South Africa and Lesotho. I have also lectured and developed course content for agricultural students since 2020. I care about clear science communication and agricultural literacy for the next generation of scientists and farmers.

I grew up partly on the Northern Cape's West Coast, which is where I fell for the natural world. Outside work I spend time in nature, look after my bonsai and grow gourmet mushrooms.
